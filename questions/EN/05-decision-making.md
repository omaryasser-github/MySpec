# Category 5: Decision-Making (Q33–Q38)

## Q33: Technical Risk Tolerance
**Question:** What is your comfort level with technical experimentation—do you strongly prefer battle-tested, conservative solutions or cutting-edge, experimental stacks?
- **Context & Guidance:**
  - Comfort with experimentation vs. stability
  - Preference for proven vs. bleeding-edge libraries
  - Acceptable failure rate in prototyping

## Q34: Primary Engineering Constraint
**Question:** What is the single most restrictive constraint in your technical work—limited time, budget ceilings, or resource/skill capacity?
- **Context & Guidance:**
  - Time availability and hard deadlines
  - Financial, infrastructure, or license limits
  - Team or individual resource boundaries

## Q35: Trade-off Evaluation Framework
**Question:** When choosing between technical paths, how do you rank speed to deliver, implementation quality, maintainability, and operational cost?
- **Context & Guidance:**
  - Primary decision criteria
  - Cost vs. quality vs. velocity hierarchy
  - How you weigh short-term hacks vs. long-term architecture

## Q36: Decision Timeline & Cadence
**Question:** What is your typical decision turnaround—do you require immediate pragmatic answers or deliberate, deep multi-day architectural evaluations?
- **Context & Guidance:**
  - Urgency level and turnaround expectation
  - Time available for proof-of-concept testing
  - Deadline and release pressure

## Q37: Decision Authority & Governance
**Question:** Are you the solo technical authority on your projects, or who are the key stakeholders you must consult, persuade, or seek approval from?
- **Context & Guidance:**
  - Solo decision maker vs. team consensus requirement
  - Stakeholders to convince (leadership, clients, security)
  - Formal approval or RFC review processes

## Q38: Past Lessons & Architectural Guardrails
**Question:** What major technical mistakes, outages, or hard-learned architectural lessons have shaped your strictest engineering rules and guardrails?
- **Context & Guidance:**
  - Past architectural or operational mistakes to avoid
  - Hard-learned rules and boundaries
  - "Never again" patterns in your development philosophy
